\begin{abstract}
A good transferred QAOA solution can leave improvements that are difficult
to propose and resolve with finite measurements. We study this regime for
MaxCut by relating trust-region radius, affine-model bias, propagated shot
variance, and acceptance power. Smaller regions improve exact-data proposals
in the diagnostic sample, but amplify gradient noise and leave smaller
decrease margins. A conditional bias--variance calculation predicts gradient
mean-square error before independent observations: pooled error on eight
fresh graphs is 1.5\% above its simulator-informed prediction. A separate
256-run factorial isolates radius-dependent sampling from stopping after
unresolved evidence. All four policies return identical vectors within each
matched run; stopping reduces expenditure without a detected gain from
adaptive sampling. Four-look endpoint replays find only 2.33\% mean Bernstein
acceptance in the fixed positive-margin cohort at the largest online
endpoint-pair allowance. The preserved 256-run revision accepts four of 288
proposals, with shaped gains below the SPSA and COBYLA means. Restoring the
original shaped comparator exposes a depth-dependent quality--cost tradeoff.
These results quantify model accuracy and decision resolution after transfer.
They establish neither an optimizer-complexity advantage nor a
measurement-only controller: the predictive diagnostics use exact simulator
quantities, and hardware validation remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) searches a family of
parameterized quantum states for a large expected objective
value~\cite{farhi2014}. Its classical parameter dimension can be small at
shallow depth, but each objective estimate requires repeated circuit
measurements. Reusing parameters from a related graph offers an appealing
way to reduce this cost. Once transfer produces a good starting point,
however, the relevant question changes: can a finite measurement budget
resolve the improvements that remain?

We examine this question for MaxCut through a finite-shot local-refinement
policy with a graph-selected anchor. Graph comparison selects a donor vector; nearby, symmetry-aligned
donors set a bounded search shape; an affine model proposes a move; and fresh
endpoint measurements determine acceptance. This construction makes four
possible sources of benefit separately testable. Descriptor selection is the
default; global transport and local-neighborhood scores are controlled
comparisons rather than presumed improvements. An objective-estimation
call, or query, is one newly sampled circuit estimate at one angle vector.
We report its shot cost and its grouping into logical batches, because fewer
calls need not imply fewer circuit executions or lower total cost.

Parameter transfer has several established foundations. Depth-dependent
patterns motivate interpolation and Fourier initialization~\cite{zhou2020},
and discretized annealing provides structured initial
schedules~\cite{sack2021}. Concentration results motivate fixed-depth reuse
within specified sparse random graph ensembles~\cite{brandao2018}.
Local subgraphs, degree parity, and learned graph representations support
graph-dependent donor selection~\cite{galda2023,falla2024}. Offline
local-tree calculations and precomputed angles can also reduce online
parameter search~\cite{streif2020,wurtz2021}. More recent approaches generate
graph-conditioned parameter trajectories~\cite{nguyen2026} or refine a
selected layer after donor transfer~\cite{patel2026}. These approaches differ
in training, preprocessing, and online measurement costs; their common
motivation makes initialization-only controls essential when assessing
additional refinement.

Graph information supplies an anchor and a search shape. The default is an
inexpensive descriptor; transport and rooted-neighborhood comparators are
supporting controls. Their role is to test the initialization premise, rather
than account for improvements that require new measurements. Detailed
structural constructions and their limitations are given in Methods.

Local modeling and shot allocation also have substantial precedent.
QR-based least squares and interpolation geometry control numerical
sensitivity~\cite{golub2013,conn2008}. Sung et al.\ use local quadratic models
and observation reuse for variational quantum
optimization~\cite{sung2020}; ANASTAARS builds noise-aware models in adaptive
random subspaces~\cite{dzahini2025}. The iCANS optimizer allocates shots to
gradient components~\cite{kubler2020}, while the QAOA method of Ha, Shashaani,
and Menickelly models both the objective and its spatial
variance~\cite{ha2024}. Stochastic trust-region and adaptive-sampling
convergence theories impose their own accuracy and sampling
conditions~\cite{chen2018,shashaani2018}. Our model is full-dimensional
and affine, with a fixed-volume graph-derived shape. The revised policy
allocates model shots predictably from the radius, reserves a first endpoint
test, and stops when the declared decision schedule remains unresolved.
These choices differ from spatial objective-and-variance modeling and
latency-aware two-stage estimation in Ref.~\cite{ha2024}. Empirical-Bernstein
acceptance alone is an established statistical ingredient, not a novelty
claim. Our variance-sensitive comparator implements this endpoint rule; it
is not a reproduction of that published trust-region method. A matched
comparison with the latter remains necessary for a claim of superiority
over variance-aware QAOA optimization.

The central scientific question is whether a measured relationship between
radius, fitting bias, shot variance, and acceptance power predicts useful
refinement conditions. We test this relationship in three stages: audit the
saved decisions and restore the original shaped comparator; isolate sampling
and stopping through a separate factorial experiment; then evaluate fixed
predictions on fresh graph instances. The bias--variance identity and
empirical-Bernstein inequality are standard tools. Their role here is to
make a conditional QAOA prediction testable, with exact-data inputs and
measurement-only decisions clearly distinguished. Matched quality--cost
comparisons determine whether this construction improves query efficiency.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Separating initialization from refinement}
Figure~\ref{fig:refinement} exposes the comparison that a refinement
claim needs. Returning the selected anchor is a complete initialization-only
policy; fitting and accepting a new point incur additional target-graph
measurements. The objective is normalized by the number of edges, with
$f_G=-F_G/|E|\in[-1,0]$, so a decrease in $f_G$ is an increase in expected cut.
Two quantities separate the two stages. The reported deficit
$D_G=100[f_G(\theta_{\mathrm{out}})-f_G^{\mathrm{ref}}]$ measures the returned
vector against the best-found reference, so smaller is better and negative
values are possible because that reference is not a certified optimum. The
incremental improvement $I_G=100[f_G(\theta_0)-f_G(\theta_{\mathrm{out}})]$
measures the returned vector against the policy's own initialization, so
$I_G>0$ exactly when refinement improved on the anchor it started from and
$I_G=0$ whenever the returned vector equals its anchor. Reporting both prevents a good anchor
from being credited to the refinement algorithm.

\subsection{Proposal quality and decision resolution}
Write $a=f(x)-f(x^+)$ for a proposal's true improvement and
$h=a-\eta q$ for its sufficient-decrease margin. A nonimproving proposal has
$a\le0$; an improving proposal with $h<0$ still fails the declared
sufficient-decrease requirement. At $h=0$ it meets the weak decrease
threshold but has no positive detection margin. On the simultaneous
confidence event, a proposal with $h>0$ may be accepted or remain unresolved.
The 288 saved revised proposals comprise 178 nonimproving points, 30 improving
points without a positive sufficient-decrease margin, 76 positive-margin
points left unresolved, and four valid accepted improvements. The margin
magnitudes and matched-rule replay determine whether unresolved proposals
were close to the rule's resolvable regime. These are proposal counts, not
288 independent graph observations.
These categories require exact diagnostics only after proposal selection;
the optimizer sees sampled means and variances. Figure~\ref{fig:acceptance-power}
freezes proposals and redraws endpoint observations with independent random
streams, so its acceptance frequencies measure sampling power rather than
reusing the observations that generated the proposals.

\subsection{Attribution and fresh-graph prediction}
The interventions in Fig.~\ref{fig:attribution} are distinct from the earlier
shape-by-rule comparison. All four policies use the same anchor, shaped
region, Bernstein test, cap, and paired random streams. Fixed versus
radius-dependent model shots is one factor. Stopping versus refitting after
unresolved evidence is the other; continuation leaves the radius unchanged
until acceptance or certified rejection. Thus this experiment does not
bundle continued sampling with the historical unresolved contraction rule.

Fresh-graph predictions are fixed before their new model observations. They
use exact circuit expectations and variances to predict conditional model
error; an approximate Gaussian coefficient model additionally predicts the
frequency of positive sufficient-decrease margins. Independent finite-shot
fits evaluate these predictions. This distinguishes a test of the proposed
mechanism from optimization on those fresh graphs: it is not a new
held-out ranking of complete optimizers. The descriptive size/depth effects

\section{Discussion}
The central mechanism is a radius tradeoff. In the original diagnostic grid,
all 32 graph/depth/shape combinations have a positive exact-data margin at
radius 0.025, while none do at radius 0.4. Small regions can therefore repair
the affine boundary proposal without making it easy to measure: the
physical gradient fitting map amplifies sampling noise as the radius
contracts, and a smaller move can leave a smaller improvement margin.
Large-radius total error that persists with increasing shots instead points
to model bias. Equation~\eqref{eq:biasvariance} separates these contributions
and specifies a numerical prediction whose calibration can be tested on
independent observations.

Three claims must be kept distinct. Earlier termination can avoid
expenditure; a positive own-anchor gain establishes useful refinement;
and a prediction fixed before new observations can establish a conditional
model of the operating regime. None implies the others. The original shaped
Bernstein policy belongs in the main comparison: its depth-three gain is
about 0.109 percentage points, versus 0.032 for the first revision, whose
mean expenditure is lower. At depth two the ordering of those gains
reverses. These are quality--cost tradeoffs on a small graph sample, not a
uniform improvement from the revised policy. SPSA retains larger mean gains
at both depths, and COBYLA combines larger mean gains with fewer mean shots
than revised shaping; graph-paired uncertainty accompanies these summaries.

The saved ledgers show where the sampling intervention actually operates.
Only 31 of 256 revised runs fit a model at a changed radius, and 28 use more
than 256 shots per point. Every revised Hoeffding run pays for exactly one
initial model and one full four-look endpoint test. Its lower expenditure
therefore supplies evidence about unresolved stopping, without testing
higher model precision at a contracted radius. Moreover, 230 of the 256
terminal decisions reach the prescribed look cap and only 26 are unable to
afford the next look. A policy-limited unresolved stop does not show that
all potentially useful allocations of the remaining budget were exhausted.
The separate sampling-by-stopping factorial makes this attribution direct:
all four policies return identical vectors for each of the 64 matched
graph/depth/repetition combinations. Their gains are 0.0365 and 0.0723
percentage points at depths two and three, while stopping reduces mean
expenditure from roughly 64000--65000 to 38000--40000 shots. Changing model
allocation alone yields no gain in this controlled sample. This conclusion
concerns the specified hold-radius continuation policy, rather than every
possible use of the remaining budget.

The four-look replay answers the online rule's power question, conditional
on the fixed proposal collection. It uses the online confidence penalty,
not a prefix of the five-look experiment with its different error
allocation. Fixing cohorts and weights across budget makes changes in
power attributable to the allowed measurements rather than changing
proposal distributions. Zero observed acceptance has finite Monte Carlo
resolution: 128 zero-success replays of a single proposal give a one-sided
95\% upper limit of about 2.31\%. They cannot empirically validate the
historical Hoeffding bound below $3.5\times10^{-6}$. That analytic bound
remains a property of its specified rule, not a universal QAOA shot lower
bound.

The predictive checks also have a precise scope. The gradient-error identity
is standard linear-estimation mathematics; its exact means, variances, and
reference derivatives are supplied by the ideal simulator. Fresh-graph
calibration finds pooled total mean-square error 1.5\% above the prediction
on the eight new targets. This pooled agreement does not imply equal
accuracy in every radius/shape/depth cell. The Gaussian coefficient model
also tracks aggregate positive-margin frequencies across the tested radii,
including the low-shot operating points, with finite Monte Carlo uncertainty.
That separate nonlinear approximation remains an empirical hypothesis.
Neither agreement supplies a measurement-only selector of useful radii or
predicts whole-run efficiency.
A deployable controller would need pilot-based estimates or justified bounds
for its currently exact inputs, would need to pay for them, and would
require a separately frozen optimizer comparison.

Variance-aware QAOA optimization already includes spatial variance models
and adaptive estimation~\cite{ha2024}. Our comparisons of Hoeffding and
Bernstein endpoint bounds do not reproduce that method, and the
bias--variance identity does not replace such a benchmark. A strong
algorithmic superiority claim would require that comparison and competent
baseline tuning. The historical validation grid is tied, so its selected
settings have no demonstrated tuning advantage. The continued policy used
for attribution retains the radius after an unresolved test; it does not
represent every possible continuation rule or reproduce historical
continue-and-contract behavior.

Transfer is a separate premise. Descriptor anchors already meet the
canonical target on all 24 graphs, and the nine canonical donor-based
policies retain their anchors after 196,341,504 shots. The local certificate
saturates on 301 of 384 depth-one pairs and every depth-two pair. Values
below one are labelled not saturated, since practical usefulness does not
follow from strict inequality. Deterministic ties remove a donor artifact
without creating information in saturated scores. Transport results must
likewise distinguish feasibility, donor changes, and quality on common
valid-target sets; an aggregate audit time cannot support a per-solver
runtime comparison.

The experiments use ideal sampling on small graphs, with no device noise,
readout bias, drift, routing, or hardware latency. Exact donor construction,
variance calculations, and reference gradients have classical costs.
Logical batches and simulator times do not estimate hardware runtime.
The finite horizon, positive radius floor, and absence of fully linear
model accuracy preclude importing asymptotic trust-region convergence
results. Time-uniform confidence sequences~\cite{howard2021} are a possible
alternative to finite looks, but their estimator, error allocation, and
quality--cost performance would require separate validation.

The attribution experiment reuses existing targets; its shot repetitions
add no independent graphs. The fresh eight-graph set evaluates a conditional
mechanism, not a new optimizer ranking, structural-shift claim, or broad
scaling law. Both old panels and the new targets use fixed banks; comparing
banks on the same targets remains necessary to isolate bank variability.
These limits are part of the result: query-efficient refinement requires a
useful model, an affordable decision, and a favorable measured tradeoff.
The present experiments test those requirements separately rather than
inferring them from initialization quality or numerical conditioning alone.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{QAOA objective, symmetries, and measurements}
For an unweighted simple graph $G=(V,E)$ with $n=|V|$ vertices (one
qubit each) and $m=|E|>0$ edges, the cost and mixer Hamiltonians are
\begin{equation}
 C_G=\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\frac{I-Z_iZ_j}{2},\qquad
 H_X=\sum_{i\in V}X_i.
\end{equation}
With angles ordered as
$\theta=(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_p,\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_p)$, we use
\begin{equation}
 \ket{\psi_G(\theta)}=
 e^{-i\beta_pH_X}e^{-i\gamma_p C_G}\cdots
 e^{-i\beta_1H_X}e^{-i\gamma_1 C_G}\ket{+}^{\otimes n}.
 \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
The minimization objective is
$f_G(\theta)=-\langle\psi_G(\theta)|C_G|\psi_G(\theta)\rangle/m$.
A computational-basis measurement $z$ produces one observation
$Y=-C_G(z)/m\in[-1,0]$. Its mean is $f_G(\theta)$. The simulator
samples the exact cut-value distribution by a multinomial draw; it does
not replace the shot distribution by additive Gaussian noise.

Each $\gamma$ has period $2\pi$ because the unweighted cut spectrum is
integer. Each $\beta$ has period $\pi/2$ in the objective because the
associated global bit flip commutes with every cost layer and preserves
the initial state up to phase. Simultaneously reversing all angles
complex-conjugates the state and preserves the objective. Reference
vectors are aligned to an anchor using these symmetries: for each of the
two global sign choices, every coordinate is shifted by its period to the
representative nearest the anchor, and the alignment with the smaller
Euclidean distance to the anchor is retained. The procedure
does not assume that every symmetry-equivalent optimum has been found.

All optimization decisions use shot estimates. Exact expectations are
computed only for reference construction and recorded diagnostics,
including post hoc objective trajectories. The reported expected
approximation ratio is $F_G(\theta)/C_{\max}(G)$, with $C_{\max}$
obtained by exhaustive bitstring enumeration at these graph sizes.
It is distinct from the largest cut observed among a collection of
samples. A single depth-$p$ circuit uses $pm$ cost-edge rotations and
$pn$ mixer rotations; a two-CNOT implementation of each cost-edge
rotation would use $2pm$ CNOTs per shot, before routing and compilation.
We do not convert this logical count into a hardware runtime claim.

\subsection{A structural certificate for objective transfer}
For a graph $G=(V,E)$ with $m=|E|>0$, let $F_G(\theta)$ be the expected
MaxCut value at depth $p$, and define the minimized objective
$f_G(\theta)=-F_G(\theta)/m\in[-1,0]$. For an edge $e$, form the induced
subgraph containing every vertex within distance $p$ of either endpoint,
and mark the endpoints as an unordered root pair. Write $\mu_{G,p}$ for
the distribution of rooted isomorphism types obtained by sampling an edge
uniformly from $E$.

The causal structure of QAOA yields the following uniform comparison.
\begin{theorem}[Transfer through rooted edge neighborhoods]
\label{thm:transfer}
For any two finite simple unweighted graphs with at least one edge, and any
shared depth $p$, define
$d_p(G,H)=\TV(\mu_{G,p},\mu_{H,p})
=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_\tau|\mu_{G,p}(\tau)-\mu_{H,p}(\tau)|$,
total variation in the convention that carries the factor one half, so
that $d_p\in[0,1]$ and $d_p=1$ exactly when the two type distributions
have disjoint supports. Then
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{\theta\in\R^{2p}}|f_G(\theta)-f_H(\theta)|\le d_p(G,H).
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
If $\theta_H$ is $\epsilon$-optimal for $f_H$ on a common nonempty domain
$\Omega$, then
\begin{equation}
 f_G(\theta_H)-\inf_{\theta\in\Omega}f_G(\theta)
 \le \epsilon+2d_p(G,H).
 \label{eq:transferregret}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
The proof below uses the established locality of fixed-depth QAOA edge
expectations~\cite{farhi2014,brandao2018}; related local-subgraph comparisons
motivate parameter-transfer heuristics~\cite{galda2023}. The formulation here
makes the finite-graph total-variation and donor-regret consequences explicit.
It gives a
sufficient transfer certificate without requiring equal graph sizes or a
vertex correspondence. It is conservative: different neighborhoods can
produce the same edge expectation, and disjoint type distributions give
$d_p=1$, for which Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} gives no information beyond
$f_G\in[-1,0]$. The factor-two regret bound can be clipped at one.
Equation~\eqref{eq:transferregret} is additionally conditional on the
donor: it presumes a value of $\epsilon$ for which $\theta_H$ is
$\epsilon$-optimal for $f_H$ on $\Omega$. The donor angles used here
are the lowest objective among six BFGS runs on the exact expectation,
a multistart local minimization that certifies no optimality gap, so no
numerical $\epsilon$ is available and Eq.~\eqref{eq:transferregret} is
never evaluated for a graph pair in the empirical sections.
Computing exact rooted isomorphism classes can also be expensive.

The global shortest-path score used in the GW construction does not appear
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer}. The two objects are not measures on a common
space: the GW coupling compares global shortest-path relations under uniform
\emph{vertex} masses $a_i=1/|V_G|$, whereas $d_p$ compares rooted local
neighborhood types under uniform \emph{edge} sampling, and no theorem here
connects a small GW distortion to a small $d_p$. Small GW distortion is
therefore a heuristic reason to try transfer, not a certificate of objective
agreement. Even a
structural comparator with an exact certificate can be uninformative on a
particular finite sample. The empirical comparison evaluates both the
quality of the selected reference and the informativeness of the scores.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}]
For a fixed edge $e=(u,v)$, let $C_e=(I-Z_uZ_v)/2$.
Propagate $C_e$ backwards through Eq.~\eqref{eq:state}. A mixer layer
acts separately on qubits and cannot enlarge its support. Within a cost
layer all $ZZ$ gates commute. Gates with neither endpoint in the
current support commute with the operator and cancel, while the
remaining gates can add only immediate neighbors. Thus each cost layer
increases the maximum graph distance from the original edge by at most
one. After $p$ layers, only the induced radius-$p$ edge neighborhood
can affect the expectation. Extra edges entirely on its outer boundary
cause no problem: they are present in the induced graph, and gates that
never meet the backwards support cancel.

The product initial state and the uniform circuit angles imply that
root-preserving isomorphic neighborhoods have identical edge
expectations. Hence, for a rooted type $\tau$, there is a common
function $h_p(\tau,\theta)\in[0,1]$ such that
\begin{equation}
 -f_G(\theta)=\sum_\tau\mu_{G,p}(\tau)h_p(\tau,\theta).
\end{equation}
For any function taking values in $[0,1]$, the difference of its
expectations under two distributions is at most their total variation.
This proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer}, uniformly in $\theta$.
Furthermore, $f_G(\theta_H)\le f_H(\theta_H)+d_p$ and
$\inf_\Omega f_H\le\inf_\Omega f_G+d_p$. Combining these inequalities
with the assumed $\epsilon$-optimality proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transferregret}.
\end{proof}
The implementation counts exact rooted isomorphism classes. Degree and
size invariants are used only to reject impossible matches, followed by
an exact graph-isomorphism test with endpoint marks. No hash equality
is treated as an isomorphism certificate.

\subsection{Structural comparison and reference selection}
For connected graphs, define
$D_G(i,k)=\operatorname{dist}_G(i,k)/\operatorname{diam}(G)$ and uniform
vertex weights $a_i=1/|V_G|$, $b_j=1/|V_H|$. A feasible coupling
satisfies $T\ge0$, $T\mathbf1=a$, and $T^\top\mathbf1=b$. Its squared
structural distortion~\cite{memoli2011,peyre2016} is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D_{G,H}(T)=\sum_{i,k,j,\ell}
 (D_G(i,k)-D_H(j,\ell))^2T_{ij}T_{k\ell}.
 \label{eq:gw}
\end{equation}
Following the regularized structural-comparison idea in
Refs.~\cite{demetci2022,peyre2016}, the independent implementation
starts from $T=ab^\top$ and applies damped entropic transport updates
with regularization $0.05$. With
$K_{ij}=(D_G^{\circ2}a)_i+(D_H^{\circ2}b)_j
-2(D_GT D_H^\top)_{ij}$, the next proposal is obtained by Sinkhorn
scaling the kernel $\exp(-2K/0.05)$, following the fixed-cost entropic
transport construction~\cite{cuturi2013}. The actual update averages the
proposal and previous coupling with equal weights. The score reported
is the \emph{unregularized} distortion of the returned coupling.

The outer loop stops at a coupling-change Frobenius norm below
$10^{-9}$ or after 100 updates. Log-domain inner scaling uses at most
500 iterations and a row-marginal tolerance of $10^{-12}$; both
marginals are checked to $10^{-8}$ before accepting a returned
coupling. The tensor $K$ is rounded to 12 decimal places before
forming the kernel. This fixed numerical guard suppresses
roundoff-scale symmetry breaking at the uniform start. It does not
certify a stationary point, a global optimum, or exact invariance
under all floating-point perturbations. Uniform initialization can
also remain uninformative on symmetric instances. Couplings,
residuals, iteration counts, and marginal errors are retained. In the
canonical study, 253 of 384 distinct target--bank comparisons terminate
without reaching the $10^{-9}$ outer tolerance. Twenty of the 24 selected
donors have such a capped comparison. Scores are reused across depths
because they depend only on the two graphs. The empirical result therefore
concerns this capped, regularized solver with uniform initialization;
it does not establish the quality of globally optimized GW matching.

The inexpensive alternative is a six-component descriptor: mean and
standard deviation of degree, its quartiles at $0.25$, $0.5$, and
$0.75$, and mean clustering coefficient. Descriptor differences are
scaled by their reference-bank standard deviations, floored at $0.1$,
and compared in Euclidean norm. The local alternative uses $d_p$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}. Neither reference choice uses the target
graph's optimized parameter label.

For scores $d_j$ to bank graphs, let
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 w_j&=\frac{\exp(-d_j/\tau)}{\sum_k\exp(-d_k/\tau)},\\
 \tau&=\max\{\operatorname{sd}(d_j),10^{-3}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The code subtracts the minimum score before exponentiation for numerical
stability; this leaves the normalized weights unchanged.
In the preserved experiments, the initial anchor $x_0$ is the first largest
computed weight in the saved bank order. In exact arithmetic the weights decrease strictly with the
scores, so this selects a score minimizer; in floating point, selection
uses the computed weights without a near-tie tolerance. Rounding-scale
score differences can therefore change the selected donor. For the local
alternative at depth two, all 384
target--bank scores equal one to within $4\times10^{-16}$, so
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} is vacuous for every pair. The selected reference is
nevertheless not the first bank vector on 3 of the 24 depth-two targets, and
those 3 targets are the only source of the difference between the local-score
and fixed-donor rows of Table~\ref{tab:results}; that difference is a
rounding artifact and carries no structural information. The revised implementation selects directly from the scores, choosing the
first bank index with $d_j\le\min_k d_k+10^{-12}$. This absolute tolerance
is part of the policy and deliberately merges score differences below it;
it removes the observed roundoff-dependent choice when Local TV saturates.
It does not create information in a saturated score. The fixed-donor control
also uses the first bank vector. Descriptor distances are the default, and
the weights determine shape only after the score-based donor is selected.
The archived source and records retain the historical selection rule.
After symmetry alignment,
only reference vectors $\theta_j$ satisfying $\norm{\theta_j-x_0}_2\le1$ are
retained for the shape, and their weights are renormalized. Define
\begin{equation}
 V=0.02I+\sum_jw_j(\theta_j-x_0)(\theta_j-x_0)^\top.
\end{equation}
Subtract the mean log eigenvalue of $V$, clip each resulting value to
$[-\log2,\log2]$, and subtract their mean once more. If the resulting
values are $\ell_j$ and the eigenvectors form $U$, set
$B=U\diag(e^{\ell_j/2})U^\top$. Then $\det B=1$ and
$\operatorname{cond}(BB^\top)\le4$. Both follow from the two recentrings
and the clip: the final subtraction of the mean gives $\sum_j\ell_j=0$, so
$\det B=\exp(\tfrac12\sum_j\ell_j)=1$; the clip confines the pre-centred
values to an interval of length $2\log2$, and subtracting a constant leaves
that spread unchanged, so $\max_j\ell_j-\min_j\ell_j\le2\log2$. The
eigenvalues of $BB^\top$ are $e^{\ell_j}$, whose ratio is therefore at most
$e^{2\log2}=4$. This scatter matrix is an anchor-centred scatter of the
retained reference vectors, not a covariance about their weighted mean, and
is used only for geometry, with no probabilistic interpretation.

\subsection{Sensitivity of the transport selector}
The transport sensitivity study reuses all 384 canonical target--bank
pairs and their saved parameter references. It changes structural
computation only: no target optimizer is rerun, and target QAOA objective
values never select a solver or a start. For entropic updates, the outer
limit becomes 1000 while the coupling-change tolerance remains $10^{-9}$.
The inner loop retains 500 scaling iterations, tolerance $10^{-12}$, and
the final marginal tolerance $10^{-8}$. Uniform-start regularizations are
$0.02$, $0.05$, and $0.1$. A separate $0.05$ multistart policy adds two
seeded random positive couplings, balanced to uniform marginals, and
retains the feasible returned coupling with lowest unregularized distortion.
The protocol fixes seed 2026091903 and deterministic offsets by graph pair
and start. The rounded cost tensor uses 12 decimal places as before.
Positive-kernel scaling recenters exponents by their maximum; comparison
with the original log-domain implementation is tested independently.

An additional solver applies the nonconvex conditional-gradient construction
of Ref.~\cite{lacostejulien2016} to the unregularized objective in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gw}. At a feasible coupling $T$, the nonconstant
part of its gradient is $-4D_GT D_H^\top$. Each search direction solves a
transportation linear program with uniform marginals using SciPy's HiGHS
interface at primal and dual feasibility tolerances of $10^{-9}$; the omitted
separable gradient terms are constant along feasible
directions. The step minimizes the quadratic objective along the feasible
segment by comparing both endpoints and any interior minimizer. The solver
uses the same three starts, at most 200 updates, and a final first-order
gap tolerance of $10^{-10}$, the gap being
$\langle-4D_GT D_H^\top,T-S\rangle$ with $S$ a minimizer of the same linear
form over feasible couplings. The lowest-distortion returned coupling defines
the comparison score. A small first-order gap is a stationarity diagnostic,
not a global-optimality certificate for this nonconvex problem. On 48 of the
384 pairs the uniform start already meets the gap tolerance without any
update, because two bank graphs have constant normalized distance row sums;
in every such pair a random start returns strictly lower distortion. Numerically
solved linear subproblems and finite precision make the reported gap a
floating-point diagnostic rather than an exact stationarity certificate. The
$10^{-10}$ gap threshold also lies below the $10^{-9}$ feasibility tolerance
of those subproblems.

Every start's coupling, distortion, residual or gap, iteration count,
feasibility status, and marginal error is retained, including finite
infeasible entropic results. The conditional-gradient routine instead raises
on a returned coupling that violates the marginal tolerance, so its recorded
couplings are feasible by construction rather than by observation. A target
has a valid selector only when all 16 donor comparisons have feasible
returned couplings; otherwise its result is recorded as unavailable.
The rule does not silently discard difficult donors. Reported transfer
means condition on the valid targets, whose counts are shown in
Table~\ref{tab:transport}; different valid-target subsets cannot support a
direct ranking. For feasible targets, the lowest structural score selects a
donor in fixed bank order. Its saved angles are evaluated on the target
only to diagnose transfer quality. Direct four-index summation independently
checks each saved distortion, and a fresh transportation subproblem checks
the conditional-gradient gap. These checks support the recorded finite
computations without upgrading them to exact GW optima.

\subsection{Geometry controls local-model noise amplification}
At a current angle vector $x$, write the trial region as
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T(x,\Delta,B)&=\{x+\Delta Bu:\norm{u}_2\le1\},\\
 B&=B^\top\succ0,\qquad\det B=1.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:region}
\end{equation}
The equivalent metric is $M=B^{-2}$. The shape $B$ is fixed during a run;
the center and radius change in response to measurements. The determinant
constraint preserves volume when comparing shapes at the same radius.
Angles are evaluated modulo exact QAOA symmetries; local models are
interpreted in the unwrapped coordinates of Eq.~\eqref{eq:region}.

For local coordinates $u_i$, the affine design has rows
$A_i=(1,u_i^\top)$. The model
$m(u)=\widehat c_0+\widehat c_{1:d}^\top u$, $d=2p$, is fitted from
shot estimates by weighted QR. The implementation always evaluates
exactly $N=d+1$ points at a common shot count, so $A$ is square and
invertible and the weighted fit reduces to interpolation of the sample
means: any positive weights leave $\widehat c$ unchanged, and no
accuracy benefit is claimed for weighting. The weights are retained
only because Proposition~\ref{prop:model} is stated for $N\ge d+1$.
If $W^{1/2}A=QR$ has full column rank,
then
\begin{equation}
 \widehat c-c^*=R^{-1}Q^\top W^{1/2}(r+e),
 \label{eq:qridentity}
\end{equation}
where $c^*$ is the local first-order Taylor model, $r$ is its bias at the
sample points, and $e$ is measurement error. Proposition~\ref{prop:model}
bounds each contribution under explicit smoothness and sampling conditions.
The smallest singular value of $R$ determines how strongly the fitting
geometry can amplify these errors.

The implementation selects $d+1$ interpolation points by ordinary
column-pivoted QR~\cite{businger1965} applied to the transpose of a candidate
design. All candidate coordinates
and basis evaluations are classical; only the selected points incur
measurements. This procedure preserves full model dimension. It does not
perform adaptive low-rank approximation and does not claim a new QR
factorization. Comparing with an unselected random design tests the value
of point placement while holding the least-squares solver fixed. Ordinary
pivoting alone supplies neither a strong rank-revealing guarantee nor an
optimal interpolation set.

\paragraph{Point selection and local-model error.}
For dimension $d=2p$, draw $d+1$ independent standard Gaussian
vectors and normalize each to the unit sphere. The repaired candidate
set contains these vectors, $0$, and the signed coordinate vectors
$\pm e_j$. Column-pivoted QR of the transposed candidate design
selects $d+1$ rows. The no-repair variant uses the original random
sphere points. Both check full rank and use the same QR solver.
No measured objective enters point selection. The implementation
records the condition number of the weighted triangular factor, not the
smallest singular value $\sigma_{\min}(R)$ that
Eq.~\eqref{eq:modelerror} inverts; a condition number alone does not
bound the inverse-design sensitivity. For the repaired design the
pivoted selection returned the same point set in every recorded trial,
so this quantity is in fact constant: at the canonical $256$ shots per
point, $\sigma_{\min}(R)=12.25$ at $p=1$ and $9.52$ at $p=2$, while the
recorded condition number is correspondingly fixed at
$1+\sqrt2\approx2.414$ and $3.992$. With $\delta=0.05$ the stochastic
term of $\mathcal E$ is then about $0.22$ and $0.38$, which is not small
against the unit range of the normalized objective.
Interpolation geometry and inverse-design bounds have an established role
in local-model accuracy~\cite[Sections~3--5]{conn2008}. The following
proposition combines a Taylor remainder, weighted least-squares perturbation,
and bounded-observation concentration for the specified noisy affine model;
full rank alone does not impose all conditions of a fully linear model.

\begin{proposition}[Local model error with bounded observations]
\label{prop:model}
Suppose $\nabla f$ is $L_f$-Lipschitz on a convex set containing
$\mathcal T(x,\Delta,B)$. Choose $N\ge d+1$ points with
$\norm{u_i}_2\le1$ and positive shot counts $S_i$ before drawing the
stage's independent observations in $[-1,0]$, each with conditional mean
$f(x+\Delta Bu_i)$ at point $i$. Let
$W=\diag(S_i)$ and let $W^{1/2}A=QR$ have full column rank. Define
\begin{align}
 c^*&=(f(x),\,\Delta B^\top\nabla f(x))^\top,\\
 a_i&=\tfrac12L_f\Delta^2\norm{Bu_i}_2^2,\qquad
 \sigma=\sigma_{\min}(R)>0.
\end{align}
For any $\delta\in(0,1)$, with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}
 \norm{\widehat c-c^*}_2\le
 \frac{\sqrt{\sum_iS_i a_i^2}
 +\sqrt{\tfrac N2\log(2N/\delta)}}{\sigma}
 =:\mathcal E.
 \label{eq:modelerror}
\end{equation}
On the same event, uniformly for $\norm{u}_2\le1$,
\begin{equation}
 |f(x+\Delta Bu)-m(u)|
 \le\tfrac12L_f\Delta^2\norm{B}_2^2+\sqrt2\,\mathcal E.
 \label{eq:uniformmodel}
\end{equation}
These statements also hold conditionally when the design and shot
counts are chosen from the history preceding this stage.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first-order Taylor remainder satisfies $|r_i|\le a_i$.
Writing the vector of sample means as $y=Ac^*+r+e$ gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:qridentity}, since
$\widehat c=R^{-1}Q^\top W^{1/2}y$ and $Q^\top Q=I$.
Hoeffding's inequality~\cite{hoeffding1963} and a union bound give
\begin{equation}
 |e_i|\le\sqrt{\frac{\log(2N/\delta)}{2S_i}}
 \quad\hbox{for every }i
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\delta$. Therefore
$\norm{W^{1/2}r}_2\le\sqrt{\sum_iS_i a_i^2}$ and
$\norm{W^{1/2}e}_2\le\sqrt{N\log(2N/\delta)/2}$.
Using $\norm{R^{-1}Q^\top}_2=1/\sigma$ proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:modelerror}. At any point in the region, the Taylor
remainder is at most $L_f\Delta^2\norm B_2^2/2$ and
$\norm{(1,u^\top)}_2\le\sqrt2$. The triangle inequality proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniformmodel}. Conditioning fixes all choices made
before the independent stage observations, so the same argument
applies to a history-dependent design.
\end{proof}
For QAOA the objective is analytic, so the required smoothness holds
on any compact region, but the theorem does not supply a small useful
$L_f$. Equal shot counts $S$ make the stochastic term proportional to
$S^{-1/2}/\sigma_{\min}(A)$; the bias term need not decrease with
$S$. Because the gradient block of $c^*$ is $\Delta B^\top\nabla f(x)$,
the recovered gradient is $\Delta^{-1}B^{-\top}\widehat c_{1:d}$, whose
stochastic error scales as $\Delta^{-1}S^{-1/2}$. Contracting the radius
from the canonical $0.4$ to the floor $0.025$ is a factor of $16$, so
$256$ times the shots per point would preserve this stochastic gradient-error
bound at the same design and shape. In the recorded runs $26.2\%$ of radius-adapting
trials are taken at the floor radius, and none of the $240$ accepted
steps occurs at a radius below $0.15$. The historical model uses
$S=256$ at each selected point at every radius and
does not estimate $L_f$ or choose $S$ by enforcing
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniformmodel}. Consequently, the proposition explains
sources of model error without certifying every fitted model as
sufficiently accurate for convergence.

The revised policy instead chooses, before any model observations,
\begin{equation}
 S(\Delta)=\max\{2,\lceil S_0(\Delta_0/\Delta)^2\rceil\}.
 \label{eq:radiusshots}
\end{equation}
For fixed design and shape this keeps the stochastic part of recovered
gradient error at a fixed scale, up to integer rounding, as the region
contracts. It does not make
the error proportional to $\Delta$. With bounded design sensitivity, the
stronger stochastic accuracy needed for a fully linear model generally
is obtained from this bound with $S$ proportional to $\Delta^{-4}$,
apart from confidence factors: coefficient noise
must be $O(\Delta^2)$ so that physical gradient noise is $O(\Delta)$.
Neither schedule removes affine Taylor bias. Figure~\ref{fig:components}
therefore measures both the sampling error about the exact-data interpolant
and the total error relative to the true objective gradient.

\paragraph{Predicting bias and propagated measurement variance.}
Condition on the history before fitting, so that the design, shape, radius,
weights, and shot counts are fixed. Write $\bar y_i=f(x+\Delta Bu_i)$,
$y_i=\bar y_i+e_i$, and $v_i=\operatorname{Var}(Y_i)$ for an individual
measurement at design point $i$. Independent pointwise streams give
$\E e=0$ and $\Sigma_y=\diag(v_i/S_i)$. With $P=(0,I_d)$ selecting
the gradient coefficients, define the physical-gradient fitting map
\begin{equation}
 L_A=\Delta^{-1}B^{-\top}P R^{-1}Q^\top W^{1/2},\qquad
 \widehat g=L_Ay,\quad \bar g=L_A\bar y.
 \label{eq:gradientmap}
\end{equation}
For the square interpolation designs used here, the coefficient map is
$A^{-1}$; the implementation obtains it by a stable linear solve, without
forming normal equations. The exact gradient $g=\nabla f(x)$ need not equal
$\bar g$. Their difference is the finite-radius affine-model bias.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional gradient mean-square error]
\label{prop:biasvariance}
Under the stated conditional unbiasedness and finite-variance assumptions,
\begin{equation}
 \E[\norm{\widehat g-g}_2^2\mid\mathcal H]
 =\norm{\bar g-g}_2^2+\tr(L_A\Sigma_y L_A^\top).
 \label{eq:biasvariance}
\end{equation}
The corresponding sampling-error mean square is
$\E[\norm{\widehat g-\bar g}_2^2\mid\mathcal H]
=\tr(L_A\Sigma_y L_A^\top)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $\widehat g-g=(\bar g-g)+L_Ae$. Expanding the squared Euclidean
norm leaves the deterministic bias term and
$\E[e^\top L_A^\top L_Ae\mid\mathcal H]$. The cross term vanishes
because $\E[e\mid\mathcal H]=0$. The remaining expectation is
$\tr(L_A\E[ee^\top\mid\mathcal H]L_A^\top)$, which is the stated
variance term. The sampling-error identity follows by replacing $g$ with
$\bar g$.
\end{proof}
This is the standard bias--variance decomposition for a linear estimator,
not a new concentration inequality or QAOA theorem. Its testable role is to
predict the magnitude and radius dependence of errors before new model
shots are drawn. We record $\norm{L_A}_2$ and $\norm{L_A}_F$ alongside the
weighted-design condition number: the latter alone cannot quantify physical
gradient sensitivity. RMS curves take the square root after averaging
squared errors, rather than averaging norms.

With constant design and shape and equal shots, the variance term is
\[
 \Delta^{-2}S^{-1}\tr(B^{-\top}P A^{-1}\diag(v_i)A^{-\top}P^\top B^{-1}).
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:radiusshots} holds its explicit inverse-radius factor
fixed, but the pointwise variances can still change with radius. It does
not control the bias term or the nonlinear step direction. For
$(S_0,\Delta_0)=(256,0.1)$, the actual schedule at radii
0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.4 is 4096, 1024, 256, 64 and 16 shots per point;
all five operating points are measured in the new diagnostic.

The prediction uses exact simulator means, variances, and a numerically
validated reference gradient. These quantities are fixed before the
independent observations used to test it, including on fresh targets, but
are not assumed available to a deployed finite-shot optimizer. A Gaussian
approximation to the affine coefficients is tested separately for positive
proposal margins because Eq.~\eqref{eq:biasvariance} alone does not predict
the nonlinear normalized boundary step or the power of its endpoint test.

Adaptive radii do not invalidate Proposition~\ref{prop:model}. Conditional
on the preceding history, Eq.~\eqref{eq:radiusshots} and the design are fixed
before the independent model samples. For a simultaneous model statement
over at most $T$ stages, apply the proposition with $\delta_t=\delta/T$ at
each possible stage and union bound after taking conditional expectations.
Model and endpoint streams are separate. Intersecting this model event with
the acceptance event gives probability at least $1-\delta-\alpha$ without
requiring independence between the two events. The implementation does not
estimate the smoothness constant or claim that this event certifies the
accuracy of every realized model.

Equation~\eqref{eq:uniformmodel} also links model quality to the
acceptance rule of the next subsection. Write
$\mathcal E_f=\tfrac12L_f\Delta^2\norm B_2^2+\sqrt2\,\mathcal E$ for the
uniform model error, let $q=\norm{\widehat c_{1:d}}_2$ be the decrease
predicted by the model's boundary step, and let $a$ be the true decrease
at that step. Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniformmodel} at the center and at
the proposed point gives $a\ge q-2\mathcal E_f$, so the detection
condition $a-\eta q\ge4b_L$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:detection}, with
acceptance slack $\eta\in(0,1)$ and confidence allowance $b_L$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound}, is implied by
\begin{equation}
 (1-\eta)q\ge2\mathcal E_f+4b_L.
 \label{eq:modelacceptance}
\end{equation}
Provided the last prescribed look is affordable, this is a sufficient joint
condition on proposal quality and measurement resolution, on the intersection
of the model and acceptance confidence events. Failure of the condition does not rule out a useful or accepted
step: the error bounds may be conservative. Neither $\mathcal E_f$ nor
$L_f$ is estimated in the runs, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:modelacceptance} provides
an analytical connection rather than an implemented test.

\subsection{Accepted steps have a finite-horizon statistical guarantee}
The affine model proposes the boundary step
$u^+=-\widehat c_{1:d}/\norm{\widehat c_{1:d}}_2$ when its gradient is
nonzero. Its predicted decrease is
$q=m(0)-m(u^+)=\norm{\widehat c_{1:d}}_2>0$. Fresh measurement streams
estimate the actual decrease $a=f_G(x)-f_G(x+\Delta Bu^+)$. At
prespecified cumulative sample sizes $s_1<\cdots<s_L$, define
\begin{equation}
 b_\ell=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4TL/\alpha)}{2s_\ell}},
 \qquad \widehat a_\ell=\overline Y_{x,\ell}-\overline Y_{+,\ell},
 \label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
where $T$ bounds the number of trials and $\alpha$ is the failure
probability for one optimization run. A trial is accepted only if
\begin{equation}
 \widehat a_\ell-2b_\ell\ge\eta q,
 \qquad 0<\eta<1.
 \label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:acceptance} shows that, with probability at least
$1-\alpha$, every accepted step in that run satisfies
$a\ge\eta q>0$, despite adaptive trial points and early stopping among
the prescribed looks. Model accuracy is not needed for this acceptance
statement, because acceptance uses fresh bounded observations.

This separation is useful but has a cost. A poor model can propose many
unacceptable steps, and a small true improvement can remain unresolved
within the allowed shots. A trial whose interval still intersects the
threshold at the last affordable look is recorded as unresolved and the
incumbent is retained. Such a decision is not evidence that the trial is
worse. With $T=64$, $L=4$, $\alpha=0.05$, and the final look
$s_L=32768$, the two-endpoint confidence allowance $2b_L$ is
approximately $0.0246$ in normalized objective units. Reaching that look
costs 65536 shots for the endpoint pair, so it is unaffordable at the
32768-shot budget; in the canonical study it is reached by 1,440 of the
21,196 trials that follow the four prescribed looks, all at the larger
budget, while the deepest look reached at the smaller budget is 8192
shots per endpoint, where the allowance $2b_\ell$ is approximately
$0.0492$. This statistical scale must be considered when interpreting
small expected improvements.

\paragraph{Simultaneous validity and detectable improvements.}
\begin{theorem}[Simultaneous validity over prescribed looks]
\label{thm:acceptance}
Consider at most $T$ adaptive trials. Before a trial's acceptance
measurements, its center, trial point, and predicted decrease $q>0$
are fixed conditional on the previous history. At each endpoint,
start a fresh stream of conditionally independent observations in
$[-1,0]$ with the corresponding true objective mean. Use only the
$L$ cumulative sample sizes specified in advance and the bounds in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound}. With probability at least $1-\alpha$, all
endpoint confidence intervals at all executed trials and looks are
valid. On this event every acceptance by Eq.~\eqref{eq:accept}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 f_G(x)-f_G(x+\Delta Bu^+)\ge\eta q>0.
\end{equation}
Early stopping or budget-based omission of a prescribed look does
not change the guarantee.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Condition on the history before one trial. For a fixed endpoint and
look, Hoeffding's inequality gives error probability at most
$2\exp(-2s_\ell b_\ell^2)=\alpha/(2TL)$. A union bound over two
endpoints and $L$ looks bounds that trial's conditional failure
probability by $\alpha/T$. Taking expectations and union bounding
over at most $T$ trials gives a total failure probability at most
$\alpha$. The argument uses fresh streams after trial selection;
within a trial, cumulative estimates may share observations.
On simultaneous validity,
$|\widehat a_\ell-a|\le2b_\ell$, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:accept} implies
$a\ge\eta q$. Stopping selects a subset of the controlled events
and therefore cannot increase this probability bound.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[A sufficient measurement scale for detection]
\label{cor:detection}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:acceptance}, fix a trial with true
decrease $a$ and predicted decrease $q$. If its last prescribed look is
affordable and
\begin{equation}
 a-\eta q\ge4b_L>0,
 \label{eq:detectability}
\end{equation}
then on the simultaneous confidence event it is accepted at or before that
look. Equivalently, for a margin $h=a-\eta q>0$, the sufficient final sample
count per endpoint is
\begin{equation}
 s_L\ge\frac{8\log(4TL/\alpha)}{h^2}.
 \label{eq:detectioncost}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
On the stated event $|\widehat a_\ell-a|\le2b_\ell$ at every look.
No earlier rejection is possible when $a>\eta q$, since
$\widehat a_\ell+2b_\ell\ge a>\eta q$. At the final look,
$\widehat a_L-2b_L\ge a-4b_L\ge\eta q$, which gives acceptance.
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound} and squaring positive quantities proves
Eq.~\eqref{eq:detectioncost}.
\end{proof}
The sufficient margin is about $0.0492$ for the canonical final look.
Even if every observed mean equalled its expectation, the canonical rule
would still require $a-\eta q\ge2b_L\simeq0.0246$ at that look. This last
statement describes the decision threshold in the absence of sampling
error, not an impossibility bound for random observations. The corollary is
a detection guarantee conditional on a sufficiently good proposal; it does
not ensure that the local model produces one. Its inverse-square dependence
on the improvement margin helps explain why a statistically valid rule can
spend a large budget without certifying useful refinement near a good anchor:
by Eq.~\eqref{eq:detectioncost}, the sufficient detection guarantee at
$h=0.005$ uses about $3.18\times10^{6}$ shots per endpoint, roughly
twenty-four times the largest
per-run optimization budget in this study, and about forty-eight times it
for the endpoint pair.

\begin{proposition}[Acceptance probability below the confidence allowance]
\label{prop:acceptancepower}
Fix the history $\mathcal H$ after selecting a trial and before drawing its
acceptance samples. Let $h=a-\eta q$ be its true acceptance margin. Suppose
all observations within and across the two fresh endpoint streams are
conditionally independent given $\mathcal H$, lie in $[-1,0]$, and have the
corresponding true objective means. For the Hoeffding rule in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:accept},
\begin{equation}
 \Prb(\text{accept this trial}\mid\mathcal H)
 \le \min\left\{1,\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}
 \exp\!\left[-s_\ell\bigl(2b_\ell-h\bigr)_+^2\right]\right\},
 \label{eq:acceptancepower}
\end{equation}
where $(t)_+=\max\{t,0\}$. The bound remains valid when early rejection or
an insufficient budget prevents some prescribed looks.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At look $\ell$, $\widehat a_\ell-a$ is a centered sum of $2s_\ell$
independent terms, each with range length $1/s_\ell$. The sum of squared
range lengths is $2/s_\ell$. The one-sided form of Hoeffding's
inequality~\cite{hoeffding1963} therefore gives
$\Prb(\widehat a_\ell-a\ge t\mid\mathcal H)\le
\exp(-s_\ell t^2)$ for $t\ge0$.
Acceptance requires $\widehat a_\ell-a\ge2b_\ell-h$.
Apply this inequality when the threshold is positive and use the trivial
bound one otherwise. Union bound over the prescribed looks, extending the
fresh streams to unexecuted looks if necessary. Removing looks cannot add
acceptance events, and independence between cumulative looks is unnecessary.
\end{proof}
For the canonical four-look schedule, every trial with $h\le0.005$ has
conditional acceptance probability at most $3.48\times10^{-6}$, even if all
four looks are affordable. This is an upper bound for the specified policy;
it does not constrain other estimators or acceptance rules. Together with
Corollary~\ref{cor:detection}, it distinguishes valid decisions from a rule's
ability to detect small improvements.

The canonical policy allows $T=64$ trials with $\eta=0.1$ and
$\alpha=0.05$. It examines each endpoint after 512, 2048, 8192, and
32768 cumulative shots. New batches contribute only the difference
from the preceding look. If
$\widehat a_\ell+2b_\ell<\eta q$, the trial is rejected for failing
the required decrease on the confidence event. Otherwise additional
shots are requested until a decision or a budget limit. Unresolved
trials leave the center unchanged. Confidence is per optimization
run; $95\%$ does not mean simultaneous validity across the complete
collection of benchmark runs. Methods and budgets reuse seed labels to support
paired comparisons; their outcomes are not assumed independent.

The canonical initial radius is $0.4$. Acceptance multiplies it by $1.5$ up to
$0.8$; rejection or an unresolved decision multiplies it by $0.5$
down to $0.025$. A predicted decrease below $10^{-12}$ terminates the
run. A fixed-radius ablation retains $0.4$ throughout. The
fixed-shots ablation uses only the final 32768-shot look, with $L=1$
in the confidence bound. This is a prespecified sampling policy,
not a hindsight-matched optimal fixed allocation. Its single look costs
65536 shots for the endpoint pair, so no run affords a second trial at
either canonical budget: at 32768 shots all 144 fixed-shots runs fit one
model and stop before any acceptance measurement, and at 131072 shots all
144 complete exactly one acceptance test, every one of which rejects. This
ablation therefore accepts no step anywhere in the study. Such costs and
unresolved decisions are retained in the records.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\paragraph{Algorithm: resolution-aware refinement after transfer.}
The revised routine \texttt{refine} takes an anchor $x_0$, a fixed shape
$B$, a shot cap $N_{\max}$, initial radius $\Delta_0$, initial model count
$S_0$, and prescribed acceptance looks. In the revision experiment,
$(S_0,\Delta_0)=(256,0.1)$, $T=16$, $\eta=0.1$, $\alpha=0.05$, and
$s=(512,2048,8192,16384)$, with radius bounds $[0.025,0.8]$.
The input shape is $I$ or the bounded descriptor shape. Its output is the
last accepted incumbent, an explicit stopping reason, and the full ledger.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Initialize $x=x_0$, $\Delta=\Delta_0$, and spent shots to zero.
Record the anchor as the zero-shot incumbent. Seed separate streams for
classical point selection, model sampling, and endpoint sampling.
\item Before trial $t$, compute $S(\Delta)$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:radiusshots}.
If the remaining shots cannot cover $(d+1)S(\Delta)+2s_1$, stop with
\texttt{resolution\_limited} and reason
\texttt{model\_and\_first\_look\_unaffordable}. Spend no model shots for
that untestable proposal. Reserving the first look assures feasibility of
one test; it does not assure detection at that look.
\item Select a full-rank QR design, sample its $d+1$ points at $S(\Delta)$,
and fit the affine model. Record radius, design, coefficients, shot count,
and condition number. If $q=\norm{\widehat c_{1:d}}_2<10^{-12}$, return
the incumbent with a flat-model resolution reason. Otherwise propose
$x^+=\operatorname{wrap}(x-\Delta B\widehat c_{1:d}/q)$.
\item At each prescribed look request fresh observations at both endpoints,
adding only $s_\ell-s_{\ell-1}$ samples per endpoint. Reject an unaffordable
batch atomically. Compute the decrease interval using either
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bound} or Eq.~\eqref{eq:bernstein}. Accept if its lower
endpoint is at least $\eta q$, and reject if its upper endpoint is below
$\eta q$. Otherwise continue to the next affordable look.
\item After acceptance set $x=x^+$, record its cumulative shot count, and
expand $\Delta$ by $1.5$ up to $0.8$. Only a certified rejection contracts
$\Delta$ by $0.5$. Rejection at the radius floor terminates with
\texttt{radius\_limit}. An unresolved decision stops immediately with
\texttt{resolution\_limited}, recording whether the prescribed looks or
remaining budget were exhausted; it leaves both the incumbent and radius
unchanged.
\item Continue to the declared horizon unless a stopping condition applies.
Return the incumbent at every ordinary stop. Rank or nonfinite-input
failures raise explicit errors rather than silently substituting a model.
\end{enumerate}
This policy distinguishes insufficient measurement evidence from evidence
against sufficient decrease. A resolution stop is operational: it says that
the declared model or decision schedule has ended without resolving
a further update under this policy. It is not a proof that all other proposals, estimators, or
allocations would fail. On the simultaneous confidence event, a certified
rejection proves $h<0$, which still permits a positive improvement $a>0$.
The preserved \texttt{optimize} and \texttt{bernstein\_optimize} routines
retain the historical contraction after unresolved decisions. They are
used only for their explicitly labelled controls and replay.

For attribution, \texttt{model\_sampling=fixed} replaces
Eq.~\eqref{eq:radiusshots} by $S=S_0$. The orthogonal control
\texttt{unresolved\_policy=continue} retains the radius after an unresolved
test and fits a new model if the same preflight reservation is affordable.
The default remains radius-dependent sampling and immediate unresolved
stopping. The continued policy differs from the historical routine, which
also contracted the radius after an unresolved test. All four attribution
cells use separate but identically seeded design, model, and endpoint
streams, giving identical first-trial observations before any policy-driven
divergence. Fixed and adaptive schedules are compared at the returned
incumbent, never by retrospectively selecting a better proposal.

The stop status and its reason are reported separately. A failed preflight
means the remaining total budget cannot fund the prescribed model and
first look. An unaffordable later look is a different total-budget limit.
An unresolved test at its last prescribed look is a policy cap, even when
additional total-budget shots remain. A certified rejection is evidence
against $a\ge\eta q$ on the confidence event, while flat-model termination
is a numerical/model threshold. None of these statuses alone establishes
stationarity or the impossibility of a useful alternative allocation.

\subsection{Follow-up across two reference banks}

Two panels use seeds 2026091901 and 2026091902. Each contains eight bank
graphs (the four families above at $n=8,10$), four validation graphs (one

validation graph matches a canonical test graph. Every target uses only its
own panel's bank. The two panels are separately seeded
replications with an isomorphism exclusion across panels, rather than
unrestricted independent graph draws.

The circuit depths are $p=2,3$. References use six BFGS starts, tolerance
$10^{-7}$, and at most 120 iterations per start. As in the canonical study,
these are best-found references, with every exact objective call retained;
no global-optimality claim is made. The seven test policies are descriptor

$\gamma=\operatorname{linspace}(0.35,0.8,p)$ and
$\beta=\operatorname{linspace}(0.35,0.15,p)$.
Initialization-only and fixed-schedule policies make no optimization calls.
All other policies start from the same descriptor-selected anchor. Only
the shaped Bernstein variant uses $B\ne I$.

Each run has an optimization cap of 65536 shots and receives 8192 independent
final-validation shots. At each depth, the validation phase compares four
combinations of model shots $S\in\{256,1024\}$ and initial radius
$\Delta\in\{0.1,0.3\}$, using both Bernstein shapes on all eight validation
graphs. This gives 128 validation runs. The selection criterion is the mean
finite-shot final-validation objective over graphs and the two shapes;
exact expectations and test outcomes are not used. Candidate order breaks

reuse seed labels; their results are not independent within a graph. The
shared local-model settings enable a controlled comparison of acceptance
rules at $B=I$. No shaped Hoeffding policy was run in this historical follow-up, so its two-by-two design
of isotropic and shaped regions crossed with Hoeffding and
empirical-Bernstein acceptance is missing one cell. The acceptance rule is
therefore varied only at $B=I$ and the shape only under empirical-Bernstein
acceptance; the shape-by-rule interaction is not identified, and that experiment leaves the shaped
Hoeffding cell untested. The revision below completes all four cells under
the revised stopping and allocation policy. SPSA retains its gains, and both SPSA and COBYLA use 1024 shots per
estimate. COBYLA retains tolerance $0.002$ but uses the common selected
initial radius $0.1$, instead of the canonical value $0.4$.
Neither baseline receives a separate tuning search.

The follow-up retains $\eta=0.1$ and $\alpha=0.05$, uses $T=16$ trials,
and prescribes cumulative looks $(512,2048,8192,16384)$ per endpoint. The
radius contracts by $0.5$ or expands by $1.5$ with the same bounds
$[0.025,0.8]$. Fresh endpoint streams are sampled after the model proposes a
trial. For an endpoint with $s\ge2$ observations, let $\widehat v_s$ be
the unbiased sample variance of individual outcomes. Assume the observations at each fixed endpoint are conditionally i.i.d.;
this holds for the ideal multinomial measurement model used here. The established
empirical Bernstein inequality of Maurer and Pontil~\cite[Theorem~4]{maurer2009}
gives the radius
\begin{equation}
 r_s=\sqrt{\frac{2\widehat v_s\log(8TL/\alpha)}{s}}
       +\frac{7\log(8TL/\alpha)}{3(s-1)}.
 \label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}
Apply their one-sided inequality to $Y+1\in[0,1]$ and its reflection,
assigning failure probability $\alpha/(4TL)$ to each endpoint and tail.
Conditioning on the history before each trial and taking a union bound
over all prescribed trials and looks gives simultaneous interval validity
with probability at least $1-\alpha$ per run. The decrease interval has
radius $r_{x,s}+r_{+,s}$: acceptance requires
$\widehat a_s-r_{x,s}-r_{+,s}\ge\eta q$, and rejection requires
$\widehat a_s+r_{x,s}+r_{+,s}<\eta q$. Other outcomes remain unresolved.
This is a direct finite-horizon application of an existing inequality,
not a new confidence bound. It preserves the true-decrease conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:acceptance} with the stated conditional i.i.d. assumption
at each endpoint.

Cumulative sample variances combine each batch's within-batch sum of
squares with the between-batch correction. Averaging the recorded variances
of batch means would not give the required $\widehat v_s$.
The comparator models uncertainty in acceptance measurements; it does not
fit a spatial variance model or reproduce ASTRO-DF~\cite{ha2024}.
The targets $f_G^{\mathrm{ref}}+\epsilon$ for
$\epsilon\in\{0.002,0.005,0.01,0.02\}$ are fixed in the follow-up
configuration before its test phase. Their hitting costs use recorded
incumbents and exact diagnostic expectations; they never stop or steer the
optimizer. Table~\ref{tab:followup} summarizes the complete test sample.
The two-bank structure and small graph sample restrict generalization;
per-panel summaries accompany pooled graph-level intervals. Each panel
draws its bank, validation split, test split and random streams together,
so a difference between panels is not attributable to the reference bank
alone.

Reusing these instances after inspecting the earlier results makes the

$256,1024,4096,16384$, with four independent sample seeds. For each fixed

Central differences of the exact objective with step $10^{-5}$ supply a
retrospective gradient reference; this is a numerical diagnostic rather than
a symbolic derivative.
Sampling error compares sampled and exact-data fits, whereas total error
compares the sampled fit with that derivative. True proposal decrease and
margin are evaluated after fitting. The exact-data control can expose a
poor affine direction or excessive boundary step even when sampling noise
is absent; more endpoint shots cannot repair that proposal.

For the first revision's power experiment, the protocol freezes all 160 proposals at
$S=1024$ and sample replicate zero: eight graphs, two depths, two shapes,
and five radii. Each receives 128 independent endpoint replays under both

$(512,2048,8192,16384,65536)$ with $T=16$, $L=5$, and $\alpha=0.05$;
the smaller cap omits the final look without changing the error allocation.
The largest pair costs 131072 shots. These are offline decision-power
experiments, distinct from the optimizer's four-look, 65536-shot-cap runs.
Both confidence rules use the same horizon, looks, cap, and error allocation. Independent
replays report acceptance, rejection, unresolved frequency, and actual
acceptance-stage expenditure, charging unresolved cases through their caps.
No proposal is selected according to its observed replay success. Binning
or ordering by retrospective $h$ is for presentation only. Finite Monte
Carlo frequency zero does not imply probability zero, and the analytic
Hoeffding upper bound concerns the declared rule rather than all possible
QAOA measurements.

Budget-indexed outputs are reconstructed from completed incumbent records.
Between recorded updates the previous incumbent is retained, including
during an unfinished measurement batch. Target fractions include the
zero-shot anchor and retain unsuccessful runs. A first-hit fraction and a
fraction whose current output meets the target answer different questions
for nonmonotone baseline trajectories; figure labels specify the quantity.
Independent output-evaluation costs are reported separately. The diagnostic
model grid uses 83,558,400 simulated observations and the endpoint replay
generates 2,684,354,560 more, reusing each frozen stream across rules and
caps. These offline diagnostic samples are excluded from optimizer curves
and are not hardware executions. Exact
references, derivative diagnostics, and replay computations are classical
analysis expenses, not free oracle information available to the optimizer.

\subsection{Resource accounting, validation, and reproducibility}
An objective-estimation call is one newly sampled circuit estimate
at one angle vector. A shot is one repeated circuit execution. A job
is one logical batch submitted by the algorithm; a model batch
contains all interpolation points, and an acceptance batch contains
the two endpoints. Additional samples at an old point constitute a
new estimation call. The oracle rejects a batch atomically if it
would exceed the remaining shot budget. It records every call,
batch, shot count, angle, sample mean, estimated variance of the
mean, and elapsed simulation time. Budget caps are limits, not an
assertion that every run spends exactly the same number of shots.

In the canonical study, every final vector receives 16384 independent validation shots,
recorded as one additional call and job outside the optimization
budget. Total operational shot cost is the optimization count plus
this validation cost. Validation measurements never select the
returned vector. The primary comparison uses the exact expected
objective at the returned vector; validation means quantify the
finite-shot evaluation available to an experimental user. The
canonical target is $f_G(\theta)\le f_G^{\mathrm{ref}}+0.02$.
Hitting cost is the first recorded incumbent's cumulative
optimization shot count satisfying this condition. Runs that never
hit the target remain failures, and their costs are retained.
The budget-capped target cost assigns the full optimization cap to failures;
for successes it uses the first hitting cost. This summary differs from actual
incurred shots when a method stops early.

Repeated seeds for one graph do not create additional independent
graph instances. The empirical summaries retain the graph as the
comparison unit, report depth and budget separately, and distinguish
successful hitting costs from success fractions. Classical
reference construction and structural preprocessing are reported
separately from online simulation time. Simulator timings are not
estimates of quantum hardware execution or communication latency.
Intervals use the percentile bootstrap~\cite{efron1993}: a custom NumPy
routine averages shot repetitions within each graph, resamples graph means
10000 times with seed 5713, and takes the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of
the resampled means. Paired contrasts resample within-graph differences.
The follow-up and attribution studies resample within each of their two
fixed reference banks. Diagnostic intervals resample whole graphs while
retaining all radii, shot counts, depths, shapes, and shot repetitions for
a selected graph. Fresh-graph and original-diagnostic intervals are computed
separately. Pointwise trajectory bands likewise retain entire graph paths.
These intervals describe variability across the observed graphs, not
uncertainty over a population of banks. They are descriptive, unadjusted pointwise
intervals, without bias correction or acceleration; a band at every radius
is not a simultaneous confidence band. Conditional replay bounds instead
quantify Monte Carlo error for the fixed proposal cohorts. They do not use
128 replays as 128 new graph instances.

The implementation uses NumPy 2.4.3 for array computation and
sampling~\cite{harris2020}, SciPy 1.17.1 for optimization and linear
algebra~\cite{scipy2020}, NetworkX 3.6.1 for graph construction and
comparison~\cite{hagberg2008}, and Matplotlib 3.10.8 for
figures~\cite{hunter2007}. The environment versions and source hashes
saved with the run data identify the implementation used for the
reported computations.

The accompanying tests compare the statevector against independently
constructed dense Pauli-matrix exponentials, verify the analytic
single-edge objective, enumerate cuts independently, and compare
weighted QR fits with an SVD-based least-squares solution. Additional
checks cover exact graph isomorphism in neighborhood comparison,
angle symmetries, coupling marginals and direct four-index
distortion, shape determinant and conditioning, budget accounting,
and deterministic repeatability. These checks establish specified
implementation properties; they do not establish empirical
superiority or general optimizer convergence.

\subsection{Scientific corrections and experiment provenance}
This manuscript replaces the earlier Gaussian-predictor formulation
and its empirical aggregates with the independently implemented
study described here. Historical aggregate evaluation, timing,
calibration, and noise results are not retained because the recovered
materials did not include the underlying experiment records needed
to reproduce them. The earlier best-of-$K$ derivation also contained
an algebraic error. Let $D_k=\norm{X_k-\mu}$ for identically
distributed draws $X_1,\dots,X_K$ with mean $\mu$ and covariance
$\Sigma$, so that $\E D_k^2=\tr\Sigma$ for every $k$. Then
$\sqrt{K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^K\E D_k^2}$ equals $\sqrt{\tr\Sigma}$,
not $\sqrt{\tr\Sigma/K}$: averaging $K$ identically distributed
squared distances leaves the root-mean-square distance unchanged and
supplies no $K^{-1/2}$ factor. No such rate is claimed here. The revised
method selects a single donor by its score with a declared numerical tie rule, but no part of the analysis credits that
selection with an improvement that grows with the number of bank
references. The new empirical record is not a reconstruction of the
historical figures.
